Hangul vowels

Hangul has 10 basic vowels and 11 compound vowels made by joining them. Every vowel is built from just three strokes: a long vertical line, a long horizontal line, and a short tick. In the original design these stood for a standing human, the flat earth and the sun in the sky.

The system is beautifully regular. A vowel with one short tick is plain: ㅏ (a), ㅓ (eo), ㅗ (o), ㅜ (u). Add a second tick and a "y" sound is added in front: ㅑ (ya), ㅕ (yeo), ㅛ (yo), ㅠ (yu). Learn four and you have eight.

Vowels come in two shapes, and the shape decides where the vowel sits. Vertical vowels (ㅏ ㅑ ㅓ ㅕ ㅣ) stand to the right of the consonant: 가. Horizontal vowels (ㅗ ㅛ ㅜ ㅠ ㅡ) lie under it: 고. A vowel never stands alone in writing. If a syllable begins with a vowel sound, the silent ㅇ is written first: 아, 오.

The two vowels that need practice

ㅓ (eo) is the vowel most often said wrongly, because its spelling in English letters misleads. It is not "e-o". It is one open sound, like the "o" in the English "office" or "song", the sound many Hindi speakers write as ऑ. Keep it apart from ㅗ (o), which is a rounded ओ with the lips pushed forward. 서울, the capital, is "Seo-ul": two syllables, s + eo, then ul.

ㅡ (eu) has no match in Hindi or English. Say "ऊ", then, without moving your tongue, pull your lips back flat as if smiling. The sound that comes out is 으. It is the vowel in 음 (eum), the thinking sound Koreans make where we say "umm".

Pairs that sound alike today

In modern Seoul speech, ㅐ (ae) and ㅔ (e) are said almost the same, and so are ㅙ, ㅚ and ㅞ, all roughly "we". You must still spell them correctly, because the spelling tells words apart: 개 (gae) is a dog and 게 (ge) is a crab.

How to learn them

  1. Learn the six plain basic vowels first: 아 어 오 우 으 이. Chant them in that order; it is how Korean children learn them.
  2. Add the four "y" vowels by adding a tick: 야 여 요 유.
  3. Then take the compound vowels, noticing that each is simply two vowels said quickly together: ㅗ + ㅏ = ㅘ, o + a = wa.
  4. Move on to syllable blocks, where consonants and vowels are put together.
