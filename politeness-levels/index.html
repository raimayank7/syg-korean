Korean politeness levels: formal, polite and casual

In Hindi we choose between तू, तुम and आप, and the verb follows: तू जा, तुम जाओ, आप जाइए. Korean has the same instinct and carries it further. The politeness is not in the word for "you", which Koreans mostly avoid, but in the ending of the verb. Every sentence you speak announces how you see the listener.

Grammar books describe seven speech levels. Daily life needs three. Learn these three and you can speak properly to anyone.

The three levels you need

LevelEnding"Thank you""I go"Use withHindi feel
Formal polite-ㅂ니다 / -습니다감사합니다갑니다Announcements, interviews, the army, news, customers, first meetingsआप + बहुत औपचारिक
Polite-아요 / -어요고마워요가요Strangers, shopkeepers, colleagues, older people you know; the everyday safe choiceआप
Casual (banmal)no 요고마워가Close friends of your age, younger siblings, childrenतुम / तू

The beginner's rule: use the polite -요 form with everybody. It is never rude. All the phrases on this site are given in this form unless marked otherwise.

Banmal: the language of dramas

Banmal (반말, "half speech") is what friends speak. It is mostly the polite form with 요 removed: 먹어요 → 먹어, 괜찮아요 → 괜찮아. Because dramas and web novels are full of friends, families and lovers, fans hear more banmal than anything else, and are tempted to use it.

Be careful. Speaking banmal to a stranger or an older person is a real insult, about as bad as calling a senior "तू". In stories, a character who suddenly drops to banmal is showing anger or contempt, and two characters who agree to "speak comfortably" (말 편하게 해요) have just become close. Now you can see those moments in the original, where subtitles cannot show them.

Honorifics: lifting the other person up

Besides the ending, Korean adds -시- (si) inside the verb when the person being spoken about deserves respect, such as a grandparent, a teacher or a customer.

  • 가요 "goes" → 가세요 "(respected person) goes; please go"
  • 어디 가요? "Where are you going?" → 어디 가세요? the same, respectfully

A few common verbs have separate respectful words, as Hindi has खाना and भोजन करना: to eat 먹다 → 드시다; to sleep 자다 → 주무시다; to be (somewhere) 있다 → 계시다. That is why "good night" to an elder is 안녕히 주무세요.

Titles instead of "you"

Korean has words for "you" (당신, 너), but polite speakers avoid them. They use the person's title or name with a respectful tail:

TailReadingUsed forExample
씨ssiEquals and juniors; after the given or full name. Like "Mr/Ms" or जी.민수 씨
님nimHigher respect; after titles and jobs.선생님, 고객님
선배seonbaeA senior at school, college or work.김 선배
아 / 야a / yaCalling a close friend or child by name (banmal).민수야

Never attach 씨 to your own name, and do not use it for someone clearly senior to you; use their title with 님.

Why age is asked so early

Since the right level depends on who is older, Koreans often ask your age or year of birth soon after meeting. It is a practical question, not a personal one. Even a single year can decide who is "hyeong" and who is "dongsaeng".

As a foreign learner

Koreans are generous with learners, and nobody expects perfection. Three habits will carry you: end sentences with 요, add -세요 when asking someone to do something, and call people by title, not by "you". Keep banmal for understanding your dramas until a Korean friend invites you to use it.
