Feelings

16 phrases in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

Feelings are where a language comes alive, and they are the words fans pick up first from dramas and web novels. In Korean a feeling is usually a single describing verb with the polite ending -요 (yo): 좋아요, "it is good; I like it". There is no separate word for "am" or "is" and often no "I" either. The situation makes it clear who is feeling it.

Take the famous 사랑해요 (saranghaeyo). Word for word it is only "love-do", with no "I" and no "you". Among very close people the 요 is dropped: 사랑해 (saranghae). The most formal form, heard in songs and proposals, is 사랑합니다.

보고 싶어요 (bogo sipeoyo), "I want to see you", is how Korean says "I miss you". And 화이팅 (hwaiting), borrowed from the English word "fighting", is the all-purpose cheer: "You can do it!"

  • 화이팅

    hwaiting

    शाबाश, लगे रहो!

    You can do it! (a cheer)

Notes

  • To ask instead of tell, keep the words and raise your voice at the end: 피곤해요? "Are you tired?"
  • Add 너무 (neomu, "very, too") or 정말 (jeongmal, "really") before any of these: 너무 좋아요, "I like it so much".
  • 아파요 covers both pain and illness. Say where: 머리가 아파요 (meoriga apayo), "my head hurts".
