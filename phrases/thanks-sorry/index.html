Thank you, sorry, yes and no

18 phrases in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

These are the survival words. With "thank you", "sorry", "yes", "no" and "please give me", a traveller can get through a whole day. Korean has several versions of each, sorted by politeness.

For thanks, 감사합니다 (gamsahamnida) is the formal form, right for shops, teachers and strangers. 고마워요 (gomawoyo) is polite and friendly. 고마워 (gomawo) is for close friends. For apology, 죄송합니다 (joesonghamnida) is the formal "I am sorry", and 미안해요 (mianhaeyo) the softer one.

Watch out for "yes". The Korean word is 네, said "ne", which sounds like "no" or "nay" to Indian and English ears. And "no" is 아니요 (aniyo). It takes a few days for the brain to accept that "ne" means yes. There is no single word for "please"; instead you add 주세요 (juseyo), "please give" or "please do", after the thing or the action.

Notes

  • In daily life Koreans answer "thank you" with 아니에요 ("it is nothing") far more often than with the textbook 천만에요.
  • 괜찮아요 is wonderfully useful: it means "I am okay", "it is okay", "no, thank you" and "do not worry", depending on the moment.
  • To call a waiter or get attention in a shop, people say 저기요 (jeogiyo), literally "over there".
  • The last two phrases are the learner's best friends. Use them without shame.
