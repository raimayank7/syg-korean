Words need company

A list of words is not a language, so each page here does more than list. The numbers page explains the two number systems and which one to use for age, money, dates and time. The family page shows how family words are used for people who are not family. The verbs page gives each verb in the polite form you can actually say, and shows how to make the past, the negative and a polite request from it.

How to learn vocabulary

  1. Ten words a day. No more. Review yesterday's ten before taking today's.
  2. Read the Hangul, not the roman line. Cover the romanisation once you know the alphabet.
  3. Put every word into a sentence. With 주세요 (please give), 있어요 (there is) and 좋아해요 (I like), almost any noun can become a sentence at once.
  4. Use the quiz. The practice page has a Words mode that tests you on all these lists.
  5. Notice loanwords. Korean has borrowed many English words, such as 버스 (bus), 택시 (taxi) and 컴퓨터 (computer). They are free vocabulary and good reading practice.

A word about dictionaries

When you look up a verb, you will find it ending in -다. That is the dictionary form, which is not used in polite conversation as it stands. Our lists give the spoken polite form first and the dictionary form in brackets in the Hindi line, so that you can both speak and look things up.
