Korean romanization: reading Korean in English letters

Is it oppa or obba? Sunbae or seonbae? Kimchi or gimchi? Pusan or Busan? Fans of Korean dramas and novels meet the same word in three spellings and wonder which is right. The answer is that there is an official system, that older systems are still around, and that names follow their own habits. This page sorts it out.

The official system: Revised Romanization

South Korea's official system is called the Revised Romanization of Korean. The government adopted it in 2000, replacing the older McCune-Reischauer system, and it is what you see on road signs, maps and in most modern textbooks. This site uses it throughout. Its main idea is to write Korean with plain English letters only, without the special marks that the older system needed.

The vowels: where most confusion lies

ㅓeo"o" in "office" (ऑ)u, o, ŏ (sunbae, hyung)

So the two-letter spellings eo and eu each stand for one vowel sound. "Seoul" is not "see-oul"; it is seo + ul. Fan spellings such as "sunbae", "hyung" and "unnie" use "u" for ㅓ because, to an English ear, that is what it sounds like. They are not wrong so much as informal.

The consonants: g or k, b or p?

The plain consonants ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ ㅈ are written g, d, b, j before a vowel, and k, t, p at the end of a syllable. The older system wrote them k, t, p, ch at the start of words, which is where "Pusan", "Kimpo" and "Taegu" came from. The cities did not change; only the spelling did, to Busan, Gimpo and Daegu.

That is why "oppa" has two p's: the word is 오빠, with the tense double consonant ㅃ, written "pp".

It follows the sound, not the letters

Revised Romanization writes what is said after the common sound changes. 감사합니다 is spelt with ㅂ (p) but said with m, so it is romanised "gamsahamnida". 한국말 becomes "hangungmal". The rules are on our batchim page.

Why names break the rules

Personal and family names are spelt as the family prefers, and many spellings were fixed long before 2000. The surname 김 would be "Gim" by the rules but is nearly always "Kim". 이 ("I") is written Lee, Yi or Rhee. 박 ("Bak") is Park. 최 ("Choe") is Choi. Some long-established words keep their old spelling in English too: kimchi, taekwondo.

What this means for you

  • Use romanisation as a walking stick for the first week, then put it down. Only Hangul shows the real sound.
  • When you meet two spellings, convert both back to Hangul in your mind. "Sunbae" and "seonbae" are both 선배.
  • When you type Korean words in English, for a search or a message, the Revised spelling is the one most likely to be understood.
  • Ready to drop the stick? Start with Hangul in a week.
