Family and people

20 words in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

Korean family words are detailed in the same way Hindi ones are. English is content with "brother" and "uncle"; Korean, like Hindi, wants to know whether the brother is older or younger, and it adds something Hindi does not: the word also changes with the gender of the person speaking.

The casual words 엄마 (eomma) and 아빠 (appa) are what children and most adults call their own parents, like मम्मी and पापा. The formal 어머니 (eomeoni) and 아버지 (abeoji) are used in respectful speech and writing, like माता जी and पिता जी. When speaking of someone else's parents, Koreans add the honorific 님: 어머님, 아버님.

For older brothers and sisters, see the four words 오빠, 형, 언니 and 누나 on the drama and novel words page; they are among the most important words in the language. Younger siblings are simpler: 동생 (dongsaeng), with 남 (male) or 여 (female) in front when it matters.

Notes

  • 친구 (chingu) strictly means a friend of the same age. An older friend is your 형, 오빠, 누나 or 언니; a younger one is your 동생. That is why characters in dramas make such a fuss about being "same-age friends".
  • 남자 친구 (namja chingu) is "boyfriend" and 여자 친구 (yeoja chingu) is "girlfriend".
  • To say "my", Koreans often say "our": 우리 엄마 (uri eomma), literally "our mum", even for an only child. Hindi does the same with हमारे पिता जी.
