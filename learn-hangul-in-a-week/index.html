How to learn Hangul in a week

Hangul is known as one of the easiest writing systems to learn. It has only 24 basic letters, the shapes follow a pattern, and the spelling is close to the sound. You do not need a month. Twenty to thirty minutes a day for one week is enough to read Korean slowly, and that changes everything that comes after.

Why learn the script first, instead of managing with English letters? Because roman spelling hides the real sounds. "Eo" and "eu" mean nothing until you know ㅓ and ㅡ. Learners who stay with roman letters build habits that take months to undo.

The plan

DayLearnDo
1Six plain vowels: 아 어 오 우 으 이 (vowels)Write each ten times, saying it aloud. Practise ㅓ and ㅡ in front of a mirror.
2Plain consonants ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ ㅇ ㅈ (consonants)Join each with ㅏ: 가 나 다 라 마 바 사 아 자. Chant the row.
3Aspirated ㅊ ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅎ and the "y" vowels 야 여 요 유Make a grid: 14 consonants down, 10 vowels across. Read every block.
4Syllable blocks with a final consonantRead the practice words. Write your own name and five friends' names.
5Double consonants ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ and compound vowelsRead ga-ka-kka, da-ta-tta aloud. Play the letter quiz until you score 20 in a row.
6Batchim: seven final sounds and linkingRead the greetings in Hangul only, covering the roman line.
7RevisionRead the food words and the drama words without looking at the roman reading.

Methods that work

  • Say while you write. Hand, eye and voice together fix a letter far faster than looking alone.
  • Use Hindi hooks. The aspirated letters are our ख, थ, फ, छ. The Hindi column on the letter pages gives a near sound for every letter.
  • Read what you already know. Brand names, K-pop group names, drama titles and loanwords such as 커피 (coffee) and 버스 (bus) are rewarding because you can check yourself.
  • Cover the roman line. From day six, hide the romanisation with a strip of paper. Peek only to check.
  • Short and daily. Twenty minutes every day beats two hours on Sunday.

What "knowing Hangul" does and does not mean

After this week you will be able to sound out any Korean word. You will not yet understand it, and you will read slowly, letter by letter, as you did in class one. That is normal. Speed comes from reading a little every day. Understanding comes from words and grammar, which begin with the phrases and the guide to sentence order and particles.

After the first week

  1. Learn the survival phrases by heart.
  2. Learn both sets of numbers to ten.
  3. Learn the eighteen common verbs in the polite form.
  4. Read politeness levels, so that you understand why endings keep changing.
  5. Switch one small thing in your life to Korean: the language of your phone's clock app, or song lyrics that you read along with.
