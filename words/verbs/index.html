Common Korean verbs in the polite form

18 words in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

In a dictionary every Korean verb ends in -다 (da): 가다 "to go", 먹다 "to eat". Nobody speaks in dictionary forms. To use a verb you remove 다 and add an ending. The ending every beginner should learn first is the polite present, -아요 / -어요, which this list gives for each verb: 가요 (gayo), 먹어요 (meogeoyo).

That one form is remarkably hard-working. Korean verbs do not change for I, you, he, she or they. 가요 can mean "I go", "you go", "she goes" or "we go"; the situation tells you which. With a rising voice it is a question, "Are you going?" Said firmly, it can even be a soft command, "Go."

The verb always comes last in the sentence, exactly as in Hindi: 저는 밥을 먹어요, word for word "मैं चावल खाता हूँ". The largest family of verbs is made by adding 하다 (hada, "to do") to a noun: 공부 study + 하다 = "to study", just like Hindi पढ़ाई करना.

Three more forms from the same stem

  • Past: add -았어요 / -었어요: 갔어요 (gasseoyo) "went", 먹었어요 (meogeosseoyo) "ate".
  • Negative: put 안 (an) before the verb: 안 가요 "I am not going", 안 먹어요 "I do not eat it".
  • Polite request: add -세요 to the stem: 가세요 "please go", 보세요 "please look".
