Words from dramas and novels

24 phrases in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

If you watch K-dramas or read translated Korean web novels, you already know more Korean than you think. Translators often leave certain words in Korean, oppa, hyung, sunbae, ahjussi, because English has no word that carries the same relationship. This page explains what they really mean and, just as important, who is allowed to say them.

The key idea is that Korean family words are also used for people who are not family, and that they depend on the speaker's gender and the age gap. A girl calls an older brother or older male friend 오빠 (oppa); a boy calls the same person 형 (hyeong). Hindi readers will find this easy: we too say भैया, दीदी, अंकल, आंटी to people who are no relation.

The second half of the list is exclamations, the short bursts of feeling that give drama dialogue its flavour. Most are casual speech (banmal). Enjoy them, but do not say them to a Korean elder or teacher.

Using them correctly

  • 오빠 is not "boyfriend". It is what a woman calls any older male she is close to, including a real brother. In romance stories a girlfriend uses it for her older boyfriend, which is how the confusion began.
  • Names take a polite tail: -씨 (ssi) is like "Mr/Ms" or जी and goes after the given name (지수 씨); -님 (nim) is more respectful and goes after titles (선생님, seonsaengnim, teacher).
  • 아줌마 can sound rude to a woman who does not think of herself as middle-aged. Shop and restaurant staff are safer called 사장님 or 이모 (imo, "aunt").
  • You will see these spelt many ways in subtitles: sunbae or seonbae, hyung or hyeong, unnie or eonni. This site uses the official Revised Romanization; see how Korean is written in English letters.
