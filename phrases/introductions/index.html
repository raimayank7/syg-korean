Introducing yourself

15 phrases in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

A Korean self-introduction follows a set order, and people really do say it this way: a greeting, your name, where you are from, what you do, and a closing line asking for the other person's goodwill. Learn the frame once and you can introduce yourself in a class, to a language partner or in a comment to your favourite author.

Two words for "I" appear here. 저 (jeo) is the humble, polite "I"; 나 (na) is the casual one. In an introduction always use 저는 (jeoneun), "as for me". The little 는 is a topic marker, a particle with no English equivalent that works rather like "तो" in "मैं तो छात्र हूँ".

Koreans often ask your age soon after meeting. It is not rude. They need it to know which level of politeness and which titles to use with you. Age in Korean uses the native numbers with 살 (sal): 스무 살, twenty years old.

Build your own introduction

Put four lines together: 안녕하세요. 제 이름은 [name]입니다. 저는 인도 사람이에요. 잘 부탁드립니다. Replace the name with yours, written in Hangul. Indian names fit Korean sounds quite well: Aman is 아만, Priya is 프리야, Rahul is 라훌.

  • After a name ending in a vowel, "입니다" is still used in formal speech; in the softer style use 예요 after a vowel and 이에요 after a consonant.
  • "잘 부탁드립니다" has no direct translation. It is a polite way of saying "I hope we get along; please guide me."
