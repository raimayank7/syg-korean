Days and time

19 words in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

The Korean days of the week are named after the sun, the moon and five elements: moon, fire, water, wood, gold (metal), earth and sun. Each name is that one syllable plus 요일 (yoil), "day of the week". So Monday is 월요일, "moon day", just as Monday is the moon's day in English and सोमवार is in Hindi.

The match with Hindi goes further, because both traditions name the days after the same seven heavenly bodies in the same order. Tuesday, 화요일, is the day of fire and of the planet Mars, like मंगलवार. Saturday, 토요일, is the day of earth and of Saturn, like शनिवार. Use the Hindi names as your memory hook.

Time words such as today and tomorrow go at the start of a Korean sentence or right after the subject, and need no "on" or "at": 내일 만나요 (naeil mannayo), "let us meet tomorrow".

Notes

  • Unlike Hindi, Korean has separate words for yesterday (어제) and tomorrow (내일), so there is no "कल" confusion.
  • 아침, 점심 and 저녁 each mean both the time of day and the meal eaten then. 아침 먹었어요? is "Did you have breakfast?"
  • To say "on Monday", add 에: 월요일에 (woryoire). Today, tomorrow and yesterday do not take it.
