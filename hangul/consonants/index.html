Hangul consonants

Hangul has 14 basic consonants and 5 double consonants. That is all: 19 shapes for every consonant sound in the language. A learner who knows Devanagari, with its 33 consonants and many conjuncts, will find this a light load.

The shapes are not random. According to the document that introduced the alphabet in the fifteenth century, the basic consonants were drawn after the shape of the mouth, tongue and throat when the sound is made. ㄱ shows the back of the tongue raised to the roof of the mouth. ㄴ shows the tip of the tongue touching behind the teeth. ㅁ is a closed mouth. ㅅ is a tooth. ㅇ is the open throat. Related sounds are made by adding a stroke: add a stroke to ㄱ (g) and you get the stronger ㅋ (k).

For Hindi speakers there is a helpful match. Korean sorts its stop sounds into three kinds: plain (ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ ㅈ), aspirated, with a puff of breath (ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ), and tense (ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅉ). The aspirated set is very close to our ख, थ, फ, छ, and the tense set is close to a crisp, unaspirated क, त, प, च. English speakers struggle to hear this difference. We have grown up with it.

Four things beginners should know

  1. ㅇ has two jobs. At the start of a syllable it is silent and only holds the place for the vowel: 아 is just "a". At the bottom of a syllable it is the "ng" of "song": 강 (gang, river).
  2. Plain consonants shift with position. ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅈ sound nearer to k, t, p, ch at the very start of a word and nearer to g, d, b, j between vowels. That is why the same letter is romanised two ways, and why the city 부산 has been written both Pusan and Busan.
  3. ㄹ is r and l in one. Between vowels it is a light flap, like the Hindi र. At the end of a syllable, and when doubled, it is "l": 물 (mul, water), 빨리 (ppalli, quickly).
  4. Korean has no f, v or z. Borrowed words use the nearest letter: "coffee" becomes 커피 (keopi), "video" becomes 비디오 (bidio).

How to learn them

Take them in the three families: plain, aspirated, tense. Say each row aloud with the vowel "a": ga, ka, kka; da, ta, tta; ba, pa, ppa; ja, cha, jja. Hold a hand in front of your mouth. You should feel a clear puff of air on the aspirated ones and none on the tense ones. Write each letter ten times while saying its sound, then test yourself on the practice page. Most learners know all nineteen within two days.

The Hindi letters in the table are a rough guide to the sound, not an exact match. Use the Listen button on the phrase pages, or a Korean speaker, as the final guide.
