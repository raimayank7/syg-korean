Greetings

16 phrases in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

Korean greetings are the first thing every learner wants, and they teach an important lesson straight away: Korean changes its words according to who you are speaking to. 안녕 (annyeong) is "hi" for friends and children. 안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo) is the polite "hello" that is safe with everyone. The longer the ending, the more respectful the phrase.

Goodbye has a twist that surprises beginners. There are two forms, and you choose by who is leaving. If the other person is going and you are staying, say 안녕히 가세요, "go in peace". If you are going and they are staying, say 안녕히 계세요, "stay in peace". Hindi speakers will find this familiar in spirit: we also say different things to the one leaving and the one staying.

A small bow of the head goes with a polite greeting in Korea, much as folded hands go with namaste. With elders and seniors the bow is deeper.

How to use these

  • 안녕하세요 works at any time of day. Koreans do not usually change the greeting for morning, afternoon and evening.
  • 여보세요 is only for answering or starting a phone call, never face to face.
  • 수고하셨습니다 is said to colleagues, staff or classmates when work ends. There is no neat English or Hindi equivalent; it thanks people for their effort.
  • With close friends of your age, drop the endings: 안녕, 잘 자, 내일 봐. Do not use these short forms with elders or strangers.
