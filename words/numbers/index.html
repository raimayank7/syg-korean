Korean numbers: two systems

26 words in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

Korean has two sets of numbers, and both are in daily use. The Sino-Korean numbers (일, 이, 삼) came from Chinese long ago. The native Korean numbers (하나, 둘, 셋) are the language's own. You cannot pick one and ignore the other, because each has its own jobs.

Use Sino-Korean (il, i, sam) forUse native Korean (hana, dul, set) for
Dates, months and yearsCounting things and people
Money and pricesAge (with 살, sal)
Phone numbers, bus numbers, floorsHours on the clock
Minutes and secondsCounting up to 99 only

So the time 3:10 uses both: 세 시 십 분 (se si sip bun), native "three" for the hour and Sino "ten" for the minutes. Sino-Korean numbers are built like Hindi and English tens in the simplest possible way: eleven is "ten-one" (십일), twenty is "two-ten" (이십), twenty-five is "two-ten-five" (이십오). Learn one to ten and the words for hundred, thousand and ten thousand, and you can say any number.

Things to know

  • Korean counts large numbers in units of 만 (10,000), not thousands. 50,000 is 오만, "five man". Indians, used to lakh and crore, adjust to this faster than most learners.
  • Before a counting word, the first four native numbers and twenty shorten: 하나 → 한, 둘 → 두, 셋 → 세, 넷 → 네, 스물 → 스무. So "one person" is 한 명 (han myeong) and "twenty years old" is 스무 살.
  • Native tens continue 서른 (seoreun) 30, 마흔 (maheun) 40, 쉰 (swin) 50; from 100 upward only Sino-Korean numbers are used.
  • Months are simply Sino number + 월 (wol): 일월 January, 이월 February. Two are irregular: June is 유월 (yuwol) and October is 시월 (siwol).
