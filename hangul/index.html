Hangul, the Korean alphabet

Four short lessons take you from the first letter to reading whole words.

  • Consonants
  • Vowels
  • Syllable blocks
  • Batchim (final consonants)

What is Hangul?

Hangul (한글) is the alphabet used to write Korean. Unlike most scripts, which grew slowly over centuries, Hangul was deliberately created. It was made in the fifteenth century under King Sejong the Great of the Joseon kingdom and introduced to the public in 1446 in a document called the Hunminjeongeum, "the correct sounds for the instruction of the people". Before it, Korean was written with Chinese characters, which took years of study and were out of reach of ordinary people. The new alphabet was designed so that anyone could learn to read.

South Korea celebrates the alphabet every year on 9 October, Hangul Day.

Why it is easy

  • Few letters. 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels; the rest are combinations of these.
  • Logical shapes. Consonants are drawn after the position of the tongue and mouth; related sounds have related shapes.
  • Spelling close to sound. Once you know the letters and a handful of sound rules, you can read any word aloud.
  • Neat blocks. Letters are grouped into one square block per syllable, so word shapes are easy to recognise.

How to go through these lessons

  1. Vowels first, because every syllable needs one.
  2. Consonants, in their three families.
  3. Syllable blocks: putting them together.
  4. Batchim: final consonants and the common sound changes.
