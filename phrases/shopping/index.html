Shopping

14 phrases in Korean, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Korean voice.

Shopping phrases reuse two patterns you already know from the food page: "... 주세요" (please give me) and "... 있어요?" (do you have ...?). Add "얼마예요?" (how much is it?) and you have most of what a shopper needs.

Prices in Korea are in won, and the numbers get large quickly: a snack may cost a few thousand won. Prices always use the Sino-Korean numbers (il, i, sam), with 천 (cheon) for thousand and 만 (man) for ten thousand. So 5,000 won is 오천 원 (ocheon won) and 20,000 won is 이만 원 (iman won). Our numbers page has both number systems.

Bargaining is normal in traditional markets and unusual in malls and chain stores, much as in India. The polite way to ask is 깎아 주세요 (kkakka juseyo), "please cut the price a little", said with a smile.

  • 이거 얼마예요?

    igeo eolmayeyo?

    यह कितने का है?

  • 너무 비싸요

    neomu bissayo

    बहुत महँगा है

    It is too expensive

  • 깎아 주세요

    kkakka juseyo

    थोड़ा कम कर दीजिए

    Please give me a discount

  • 다른 색 있어요?

    dareun saek isseoyo?

    क्या कोई और रंग है?

    Do you have another colour?

  • 더 큰 거 있어요?

    deo keun geo isseoyo?

    क्या इससे बड़ा है?

    Do you have a bigger one?

  • 더 작은 거 있어요?

    deo jageun geo isseoyo?

    क्या इससे छोटा है?

    Do you have a smaller one?

  • 입어 봐도 돼요?

    ibeo bwado dwaeyo?

    क्या मैं पहनकर देख सकता हूँ?

    igeo salgeyo

    geunyang boneun geoyeyo

    kadeu dwaeyo?

    क्या कार्ड चलेगा?

    Can I pay by card?

    yeongsujeung juseyo

    gyohwanhal su isseoyo?

    क्या इसे बदला जा सकता है?

    Can I exchange it?

  • 예뻐요

    yeppeoyo

    सुंदर है

    It is pretty

Useful shop words

  • 이거 (igeo) "this", 그거 (geugeo) "that, near you", 저거 (jeogeo) "that, over there". Pointing and saying 이거 주세요 works everywhere.
  • 원 (won) is the currency. 세일 (seil) is a sale, borrowed from English.
  • Shop staff greet you with 어서 오세요 (eoseo oseyo), "welcome, come in". A nod and 안녕하세요 is the right reply.
